Every app for reading things later ends the same way. Two hundred things in a pile, a little guilt each time you open it, and eventually you stop opening it.

Pluck lets the pile shrink on its own, and never throws anything away.

## The rule

If you don't touch something in your Inbox or Later for 30 days, it moves to the Archive.

Touching means opening it, editing it, tagging it or adding it to a list. Any of those starts the 30 days again.

Two things never move: anything marked Done, and anything in a list. A list is a plan, so Pluck leaves it alone.

## What you see

A row that's been sitting a while shows how long it's been untouched. Tap that and you keep it: "Kept. The 30 days start again."

Later tells you how many go this week, and that they go into the Archive, not away.

## The Monday email

Every Monday at 06:00 your time, Pluck emails you what's about to move, what's due this week and what's been in your Inbox longest. Anything about to move has Keep and Let it go, and the buttons work for a week without signing in. It's picked by simple rules, not AI. If there's nothing to say, nothing is sent.

Pick a different day, or turn it off, in Settings.

## Change it

Settings, then Expiry and archive. Choose 14, 30, 60 or 90 days, or Never. Leave Later out if you'd rather only the Inbox moves.

If the pile is already beyond help, there's a button for that too. Archive it all. No, really. It asks once, tells you how many, and says it again: nothing is deleted.

## The Archive is not a bin

Everything in the Archive is still searchable, titles, notes, tags and page text. Restore puts any item back. Filter by Expired to see what moved on its own.

Deleting is a separate thing. A deleted item sits in the bin for 30 days before it's gone. Your AI can only delete if you grant that when you connect it, and most AI tools never ask.

Gone. Well, archived.
