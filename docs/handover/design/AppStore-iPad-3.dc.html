The case for a shorter reading list

By Niamh Doyle readinghabits

Most reading lists fail the same way. They grow faster than anyone could read them, and the length turns into guilt. Every new link is a small promise, and the promises pile up.

A short list is one you can finish. Pick five. Read them. Then pick five more. Let the rest go somewhere quiet, where you can find them if you ever want them.

The trick is not reading faster. It is deciding sooner. A list with an end has a shape, and a shape is easier to keep than a pile.
