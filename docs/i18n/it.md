# Italian (it) glossary

How Pluck says its product words in Italian, and why. Use these in every catalog, the store listings and support replies. "Pluck" the brand stays in Latin letters everywhere.

## Product words

| Pluck (verb), pluck it | **prendere**, *Prendilo* | Plain, short, everyday Italian for "grab it", and it works in every tense the copy needs (*prendi*, *preso*, *cosa ho preso*). *Salvare* is the word Pluck avoids. *Cogliere* is the literal "pluck a flower", but it sounds poetic in UI (*Coglilo*), invites schoolyard jokes, and *Colto.* also reads as "cultured". *Acchiappare* is too childish for legal text. | Pocket and Safari say *salva* / *aggiungi*; we deliberately do not |

## Tone

- **Informal *tu*** everywhere, including Privacy and Terms (as Pluck's English talks to "you"). Imperatives in buttons: *Prendilo*, *Tienilo*, *Archivia*.
- **Avoid gendering the reader.** Prefer *se è la prima volta* over *se sei nuovo/nuova*, *Grazie per l'iscrizione* over *Grazie per esserti iscritto*.
- **Short and plain.** Pluck's English is dry and a little funny (*Gone. Well, archived.* → *Sparito. Anzi, archiviato.*; *No, really.* → *Sì, davvero.*). Keep the joke when Italian has one; drop puns that do not survive (*Pluck off* → *Lascia stare*, *Pluck it, I'm in* → *Va bene, ci sto*).
- **No exclamation marks, no emoji.** Not even where Italian marketing usually puts one.
- **Expiry copy** always says the Item moves to the Archive and nothing is deleted: *passa nell'Archivio … Non viene eliminato nulla.*
- **Words we do not use** where the English avoids them: *salvare/salvato* for capture (we use it only where the English itself says "save", such as the Save button or a note's autosave), *segnalibri* (only for browser bookmark files, as in English), *raccolta/collezione*, *intelligente*, *magico*, *basato sull'IA*.

## Capitalisation

- Sentence case for titles and buttons, as Italian always does.
- Capitalise the names of places and Kinds when they are used as names: *Inbox*, *Più tardi*, *Archivio*, *Cestino*, *Da leggere*, *Da fare*, *Nota*, *Oggetto*, and screen names (*Impostazioni*, *Strumenti di IA*).
- Do not capitalise *elemento*, *tag*, *lista* in running text, even where English writes "Item" or "List": Italian never capitalises common nouns.
- Days and months are lowercase (*lunedì*, *24 ottobre 2026*).

## Punctuation

- Quotes: curly double quotes “…” in UI and email copy, matching the English. Straight quotes stay where English uses them in text meant to be pasted (prompts) and in plain-text email.
- Apostrophes are straight (') in the catalogs. In ICU messages never put an apostrophe right before `{` or `#`: it starts an ICU quote. Rephrase instead (*l'elemento* not *l'{name}*).
- Ellipsis: the single character …
- Units: a space before the unit (*12 min*, *3 h*, *300 ms*).
- Keyboard keys stay as English (*Alt+Shift+P*, *Enter*, *Esc*), as the task rules say; Chrome on an Italian keyboard shows *Maiusc*, which is close enough for users to recognise.

## Plurals

Italian uses `one` and `other` (CLDR also has `many` for compact millions, which Pluck never shows). Past participles agree with the subject: *# elemento spostato*, *# elementi spostati*; *Presa # scheda*, *Prese # schede*.
