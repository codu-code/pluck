# German (de) glossary

How Pluck's product words are said in German, and why. Every German catalog (`packages/i18n/messages/*/de.json`) and the store listing (`docs/store/de.json`) follow this table.

## Tone

- **Du, never Sie.** Modern consumer apps in German say du: Apple's German UI and support pages („Füge Tags hinzu“), Todoist, Notion, Spotify. Pluck's English is warm and direct; Sie would make it stiff. Du, dein, dir are lowercase in running text, as Apple writes them.
- **Imperatives use the short form**: „Pflück es“, „Sortier es“, „Prüf die Verbindung“, „Tippe auf Teilen“ (keep the -e where the short form sounds odd: tippe, wähle when it reads better). This is how German apps talk.
- **Short buttons.** German runs about 30% longer than English, so buttons are infinitives or two words („Pflücken“, „Behalten“, „Noch mal versuchen“), tab and status names one word.
- **Jokes are kept, not translated.** „Gone. Well, archived.“ is „Weg. Na ja, archiviert.“; „0 nags“ is „0 Druck“ („Mahnungen“ are payment reminders); „picked by“ on public pages is „gepflückt von“; the cookie buttons „Pluck it, I'm in“ and „Pluck off“ are „Klar, ich bin dabei“ and „Finger weg“ (the pun on pluck has no German twin; „Finger weg“ keeps the cheek and the hands). Privacy quotes the buttons exactly.
- **No exclamation marks, no emoji**, as in English.
- **Expiry copy** always says the Item moves „ins Archiv“ and „nichts wird gelöscht“.

## Capitalization and punctuation

- German capitalizes all nouns, so product words are capitalized anyway: Eingang, Später, Archiv, Liste, Tag, Eintrag, Notiz. Status names used as places stay capitalized: „nach Später“.
- Quotes: German low-high quotes „…“, nested ‚…‘. Apostrophe ’ (das war’s).
- Ellipsis: „Wird gepflückt …“ with a space before the ellipsis, per Duden.
- Headlines keep the full stop where the English has one („Pflück es.“, „Sortier es später.“).
- Times: 24-hour „06:00“; dates „24. Oktober 2026“; units „12 Min.“, „3 Std.“.
- Placeholders keep their names; word order moves them where German needs it („{ago} im Web gepflückt“).

## Notes for the store listing

- App Store name „Pluck: Später lesen“: „später lesen“ is the term German press and users use for the whole category (appgefahren.de, iphone-ticker.de: „Später-lesen-Dienst“).
- Keywords are what German users search, not a translation of the English: „leseliste“ (Safari's word), „lesezeichen“, „merkliste“, „links speichern“, „artikel“, „offline“, plus the English „read later“ and „reader“, which German users also type. Words already in the name and subtitle are left out, since Apple indexes those.
- „links speichern“ and „lesezeichen“ are allowed in keywords only: they are search terms, never UI copy.
