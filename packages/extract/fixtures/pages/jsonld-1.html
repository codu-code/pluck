Albums of ordinary days

Every printed photograph is an argument about what mattered. The family albums from the seventies are mostly birthdays and beaches, and almost nothing of ordinary weekdays, which are the days we now miss.

Library books carry the notes of strangers. A pencil underline in a chapter on bridges, a receipt used as a bookmark, a question in the margin that nobody ever answered.

A walk through the old town takes an hour if you do not stop, and most of a day if you do. The market opens at eight, the bakery sells out by ten, and the bridge is quietest just before the evening light.
