Five habits of teams that finish

1. Most teams do not have a tooling problem

Most teams do not have a tooling problem. They have a finishing problem. Work starts quickly and stalls somewhere between the second review and the release note, and nobody can say who is holding it.

2. The fix is not another dashboard

The fix is not another dashboard. It is a short list of things that are allowed to be in progress at once, written where everyone can see it, and a habit of saying out loud when something leaves the list.

3. The release checklist fits on one card

The release checklist fits on one card. Tests green, migration reviewed, rollback written down, and one person who is awake for the next hour and knows they are on call for it.

4. Side projects ship when the scope is smaller tha

Side projects ship when the scope is smaller than your patience. Pick one boring stack, cut every feature that is not the core loop, and give yourself a Sunday night deadline that you tell someone about.

5. Writing for engineers who skim means leading wit

Writing for engineers who skim means leading with the decision. Put the recommendation in the first sentence, the evidence in the second paragraph, and the history at the end where only the curious will find it.
