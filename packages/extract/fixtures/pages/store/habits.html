The surprising science of habits

By Ciara Walsh

Most of what you do in a day, you did yesterday. Not because you chose it again, but because the choosing stopped some time ago. That is what a habit is: a decision your brain no longer bothers to make.

The surprising part is how little willpower has to do with it. People who keep good habits are not better at resisting. They have arranged their days so there is less to resist.

The cue does the work

Every habit starts with a cue: a time, a place, a feeling, the person you are with. The cue is the trigger, and it fires long before you notice you have decided anything. Change the cue and the habit often goes with it.

That is why moving house is such a good time to start running, and such a bad time to keep a diet. The old cues are gone. For a few weeks, every choice is a real choice again.

Small is not a compromise

The habits that last start so small they feel silly. One push-up. One page. Shoes by the door. Small is not the warm-up for the real habit. Small is the habit. The size comes later, on its own, once showing up is automatic.

When it slips

Missing once does almost nothing. Missing twice starts a new habit: the habit of not doing it. The rule that works for most people is simple. Never miss twice.
